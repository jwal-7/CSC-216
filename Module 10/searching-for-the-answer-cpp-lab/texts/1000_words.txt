The apartment came with a room the landlord refused to discuss. It was the smallest of the three, windowless, with a single bare bulb and walls that had been painted over so many times the surface felt soft. The previous tenant had used it for storage. The landlord simply said, “You can keep your boxes in there if you want. Most people do.”

Daniel moved in at the beginning of winter. He put his winter coats and two sealed cartons in the small room and closed the door. For the first week he forgot it existed.

On the eighth night he woke to the sound of soft conversation coming from behind that door. Two voices, low and indistinct, as if people were speaking carefully so as not to be overheard. He stood in the hallway and listened. The voices continued for several minutes, then stopped the moment his hand touched the doorknob.

Inside, the room was exactly as he had left it. The coats hung motionless. The cartons remained sealed. The air was colder than the rest of the apartment.

The conversations returned every few nights. Always the same soft cadence, never clear enough to understand. Daniel began leaving a digital recorder running inside the room while he slept. In the morning the device showed hours of near-silence interrupted by brief segments of the same muffled speech. When he increased the volume, the words remained just beyond comprehension, as if the speakers were deliberately staying at the edge of hearing.

He asked the landlord about previous tenants. The man grew uncomfortable and said only that the unit had a high turnover. “Some people don’t like the quiet,” he added, which made little sense.

By the third week Daniel could no longer sleep through the nights. The voices had grown slightly clearer. He began to recognize individual tones—one higher, one lower—though the language still eluded him. Once he thought he heard his own name spoken, but when he replayed the recording the moment remained ambiguous.

On the twenty-third night he opened the door while the voices were active. The room was empty. The coats still hung in place. Yet the conversation continued for several seconds after the door stood wide, as if the speakers had not yet noticed they were observed. Then it stopped.

Daniel packed what he could carry and left the same morning. He did not retrieve the cartons or the coats. When the landlord asked why he was breaking the lease, Daniel only said that the small room was not suitable for storage after all.

The apartment was re-rented within two weeks. The new tenant, according to the building’s group chat, lasted eleven days before moving out without explanation. No one has mentioned the small room since.
